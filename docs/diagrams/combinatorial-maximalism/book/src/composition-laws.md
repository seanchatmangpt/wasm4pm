# Composition laws

The atlas is useful only when diagrams can be combined without semantic drift. The following laws govern cross-view composition.

## Law 1: One primary question per diagram

A diagram may contain supporting information, but it must optimize for one question: order, ownership, type structure, deployment, data cardinality, strategy, allocation, or diagnosis. When a diagram tries to answer all questions, it becomes an untestable poster.

## Law 2: No arrow without a morphism

Every directed relationship must correspond to a source-grounded call, data carrier, causal hypothesis, dependency, or explicitly labeled target-state morphism. Similar names do not create an edge.

## Law 3: No actuation without BRCE

Any pattern depicting execution, release, deployment, persistence, or publication must include the authority boundary or link to a pattern that does. A diagram that routes around the broker is not a simplification; it is a different and unlawful architecture.

## Law 4: Evidence forms remain distinct

Tests, formal proofs, receipts, semantic replay, and cryptographic chain verification overlap but are not substitutes. The Venn, requirement, packet, and state patterns preserve those distinctions.

## Law 5: Current and target rails do not silently converge

The executable TypeScript session rail and the Rust `interview::*` component rail remain distinct until a composition root and end-to-end evidence prove convergence. Target diagrams must say target.

## Law 6: Quantitative views declare their denominator

Pie, Sankey, XY, radar, treemap, and quadrant charts require a dataset or must be labeled diagnostic. A number without provenance is a design prompt, not a measurement.

## Law 7: Rendering is not runtime proof

Successful Mermaid parsing proves only that the source belongs to a grammar accepted by that renderer. It does not prove that any depicted component, transition, policy, or standing exists.
