## 3. Actual Capability

Implements J. R. Quinlan's First-Order Inductive Learner (FOIL) algorithm for top-down induction of first-order Horn clauses using greedy information gain search (Quinlan 1990), as implemented in [ilp.rs](file:///Users/sac/wasm4pm/crates/wasm4pm-cognition/src/breeds/ilp.rs).

- **Inputs**: Expects:
  - Positive examples represented by `pos:<atom>` facts.
  - Negative examples represented by `neg:<atom>` facts.
  - Background knowledge represented by `bg:<atom>` facts.
- **Outputs**: Returns a `BreedOutput` where `selected` is the first learned clause string (e.g. `"daughter(V0,V1) :- female(V0), parent(V1,V0)"`). The `facts` array contains the learned rules mapped to `ilp:rule:<index>`. The `inference_trace` contains steps detailing example parsing (`load-example`), literal generation and evaluation (`propose-literal`, `score-gain`), literal selection (`add-literal`), covered positive removal (`cover-remove`), clause creation (`emit-clause`), and final statistics (`decision`).
- **State Touched**: Stateless, runs in isolated linear memory.
- **Error Behavior**: Triggers error if the rule builder cannot separate positive and negative examples within the body length budget (4) or clause count budget (8). Rejects more than 64 background facts, 32 training examples, or 256 candidate literals in preconditions.
- **Determinism**: Fully deterministic. Input facts are sorted lexicographically, and candidate literals are evaluated and selected in a deterministic order. Tie-breaks compare candidate literal string representations alphabetically, ensuring identical clauses and output hashes.

## 4. Expected Semantics

- **Normal Case**: Induces Horn clauses by repeatedly adding body literals that maximize information gain over binding-tuples until no negative bindings are covered, then removes covered positive examples and repeats.
- **Empty/Minimal Case**: Preconditions reject inputs lacking positive examples or background facts.
- **Malformed Case**: Rejects mixed target predicates (different names or arities) or atoms with invalid syntax.
- **Boundary Case**: Reaches the complexity caps of exactly 64 background facts, 32 total examples, 256 candidates, clause budget of 8, or body length of 4.
- **Non-Trivial Representative Case**:
  - **Quinlan 1990 FOIL Daughter Task**: Background relations `parent/2` and `female/1`. Positives: `daughter(mary,ann)` and `daughter(eve,tom)`. Negatives: `daughter(tom,ann)`, `daughter(eve,ann)`, `daughter(ian,tom)`, and `daughter(ann,mary)`.
    - FOIL starts with head `daughter(V0,V1)` and variable binding-tuples matching example constants.
    - Generates candidate literals over variables $V_0, V_1$.
    - Evaluates information gain: `female(V0)` yields highest gain because it separates female daughters from male sons.
    - Resolves variables: `parent(V1,V0)` is added next to link parents to daughters.
    - The clause `daughter(V0,V1) :- female(V0), parent(V1,V0)` now covers zero negative bindings. It covers both positive examples, which are removed. The cover-remove loop terminates, emitting 1 pure clause.
  - **Information Gain Discrimination**: Given positive target `target(1)`, `target(2)`, `target(3)` and negative `target(4)`. Background predicates are `good` (true on 1, 2, 3) and `distractor` (true on 1, 2, 4). The solver calculates the information gain for both. Since `good(V0)` covers all positives and zero negatives, it yields a higher gain than `distractor(V0)` (which covers a negative), selecting `target(V0) :- good(V0)`.
