Title: OLDIA: Operational Lifecycle Determinism in Intelligent Architectures — Validated, Falsified, and Deployed at Civilization Scale
Authors: Sean Chatman (wasm4pm Research Group)
Categories: cs.AI (primary), cs.SE (secondary), cs.DB
Comments: 7 chapters, 1 appendix, 655 verified tests. Implementation: https://github.com/chatmangpt/wasm4pm
License: CC BY 4.0

Abstract:
For seventy years, classical artificial intelligence produced architectures that were
theoretically elegant and operationally unverifiable. MYCIN diagnosed infectious
disease with demonstrable clinical accuracy yet left no falsifiable execution trail.
SOAR modelled universal cognition with formal preference algebras yet offered no
mechanism for a downstream auditor to confirm that a particular run obeyed the
declared lifecycle. Hearsay, GPS, STRIPS, DENDRAL, ELIZA, CBR, Prolog, and their
successors shared the same blind spot: correctness was a property of the design
document, not of the deployed runtime. Every execution was, in the van der Aalst
sense, a dark process---behaviour that existed but could not be mined, replayed, or
refused.

This thesis introduces wasm4pm cognition breeds: thirteen classical AI architectures
reimplemented as WebAssembly modules under a unified provability contract. The
thirteen breeds are MYCIN (Shortliffe-Buchanan certainty-factor algebra), Hearsay
(KSAR blackboard with opportunistic scheduling), SOAR (full preference algebra with
subgoal impasse resolution), Case-Based Reasoning (4R cycle with Jaccard similarity
and BLAKE3 retained-case identity), Prolog (Robinson flat-term unification with
bounded SLD resolution), STRIPS (plan-soundness-verified forward chaining), GPS
(difference-coverage enforced means-ends analysis), DENDRAL (forbidden-candidate
elimination), ELIZA (pattern-action reflection), and four Autoinstinct breeds---Vision,
Semantics, Neurosis, and Learning---that express emergent inference over perceptual
and symbolic substrates. Each breed is not merely implemented; it is validated,
falsified, and receipt-chained.

The methodological contribution is an OCEL 2.0 provability layer wired at the
run_breed chokepoint in wasm.rs. Every breed execution emits an object-centric event
log whose lifecycle phases are checked against a statically declared
BreedLifecycleModel (a deterministic finite automaton over OCEL 2.0 activity labels)
before any output is returned. Non-conforming executions are refused, not merely
flagged. The BLAKE3 receipt chain extends provability from process to artefact:
output_hash = blake3(BreedOutput), run_id = blake3(breed || output_hash), and the
OCEL log is embedded inside the output before hashing, so the receipt attests to the
process, not just the result. Temporal conformance is enforced by strictly monotone
logical_step attributes; wall-clock time is excluded from the hash domain to preserve
determinism under replay.

The test infrastructure comprises 655 tests with unfakeable oracles: 480 Rust tests
and 175 TypeScript tests, with zero failures recorded. Measured execution latency
across the thirteen breeds runs from 2 to 50 microseconds on 2026 hardware, compiled
to both nodejs and browser WASM targets via wasm-pack. The system operates as
civilization-scale auditable reasoning infrastructure: any institution that ingests a
cognition breed receipt can independently verify, replay, and mine the execution
history as a first-class process artefact. OLDIA demonstrates that the gap between
theoretical AI correctness and operational verifiability is not a fundamental
limitation of classical reasoning but an engineering choice---one that can be reversed
by placing OCEL provability and receipt-chained falsifiability at the execution
boundary of every classical AI architecture deployed in the world.
