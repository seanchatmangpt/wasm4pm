\chapter{The Platform as an Economic Object}
\label{ch:platform}

\section{What wasm4pm actually is}

wasm4pm is two layers with distinct economic properties:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item A \textbf{Rust/WASM core} containing 60 registered process mining
        algorithms --- discovery (DFG, heuristic, inductive), conformance
        checking, performance analysis, and object-centric querying ---
        compiled to WebAssembly. Deterministic by construction: the same
        input produces a bit-exact output, with all randomness seeded and
        all hash-map iteration sorted.
  \item A \textbf{TypeScript monorepo} of eleven packages that wrap the core
        with a professional CLI (\texttt{wpm}), Zod-validated configuration,
        an execution planner with four deployment profiles, OpenTelemetry
        observability, and a Supabase persistence layer.
\end{enumerate}

Three architectural decisions create the commercial surface area this
document explores.

\section{Determinism as a product feature}

Most analytics platforms treat reproducibility as a best effort. wasm4pm
treats it as a merge gate: same input, bit-exact output, enforced in CI.
Commercially, determinism converts an analytical claim into an
\emph{auditable artifact}. A compliance officer does not have to trust that
the conformance score was 0.94; they can re-run the analysis and obtain the
identical bytes. Determinism is the precondition for everything in
Chapter~\ref{ch:eaas}.

\section{The receipt chain}

Every \texttt{wpm run} emits a BLAKE3 receipt to
\texttt{.wasm4pm/receipts/} containing the run identifier, the command, a
hash of every input, a hash of every output, and the execution status. The
receipts compose into a chain: the output hash of one analysis becomes the
input hash of the next. The chain is the platform's most defensible asset
because it is \emph{accumulated state} --- a competitor can reimplement an
algorithm in a weekend, but cannot reproduce a customer's two-year evidence
history.

\section{WebAssembly as a distribution strategy}

Compiling to WASM collapses the deployment matrix. One artifact runs in the
browser, in Node.js, on edge runtimes (Cloudflare Workers, Fastly), and
inside any host language with a WASM runtime (Python, Go, JVM,~.NET). The
size-profiled builds --- mobile ($\sim$500\,KB), IoT ($\sim$1\,MB), edge
($\sim$1.5\,MB), fog ($\sim$2\,MB), and full browser ($\sim$3.5\,MB) ---
mean the same algorithm catalog can be sold into radically different
deployment contexts without porting cost. Chapter~\ref{ch:edge} prices this.

\section{The unit of value}

The framing assumption for every model that follows:

\begin{quote}
\itshape Algorithms are commodities; pm4py gives away a superset of this
catalog for free. The sellable unit is the receipt --- the verifiable,
portable, machine-checkable proof that a specific analysis ran on specific
data and produced a specific result, on infrastructure the customer
controls.
\end{quote}
