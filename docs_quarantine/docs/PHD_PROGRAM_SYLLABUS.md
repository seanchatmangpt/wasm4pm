# PhD Program Syllabus: Autonomous Process Systems & Combinatorial Maximalism

**Degree:** Ph.D. in Process Intelligence and Autonomous Systems Architecture
**Institution:** Institute for Advanced Process Studies
**Program Duration:** 4 Years (8 Semesters)

## Program Overview

This doctoral program is designed to produce leading researchers and principal engineers capable of building, auditing, and deploying nanosecond-scale autonomous process systems. The curriculum synthesizes seven braided academic disciplines: Design Science Research (DSR), process-evidence science, actor-oriented route execution, proof-carrying admissibility, bounded reasoning, high-performance WASM execution, and socio-technical civic deployment.

Students will master the complete ecosystem: **`wasm4pm`**, **`Prolog8`**, **`MCPP` (Multi-Agent Combinatorial Process Protocol)**, and **`MCP+`**, culminating in a dissertation framed by the Vision 2030 closed-loop paradigm.

---

## Year 1: Foundations of Evidence and Logic

**Objective:** Establish the theoretical baselines of process mining, bounded logic, and the Design Science Research methodology.

### Semester 1: The Process Evidence Canon
*   **Course 101: Introduction to Process Mining & Conformance Checking**
    *   *Readings:* van der Aalst (2012, 2022); Carmona et al. (2018).
    *   *Topics:* Event logs, Alpha/Inductive miners, DFG heuristics, alignment-based conformance.
*   **Course 102: Object-Centric Process Mining (OCPM) & POWL**
    *   *Readings:* van der Aalst (2019); OCEL 2.0 Standard; Kourani & van Zelst (2023).
    *   *Topics:* Bridging 2D traces to N-dimensional object intersections, Partially Ordered Workflow Language (POWL).
*   **Course 103: Design Science Research (DSR) & System Governance**
    *   *Readings:* Hevner et al. (2004); Peffers et al. (2007); ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288.
    *   *Topics:* Building auditable artifacts, life-cycle control, TRL scoring, formulation of research cycles.

### Semester 2: Bounded Reasoning and Proof-Carrying Execution
*   **Course 104: Foundations of Bounded Logic**
    *   *Readings:* Abiteboul & Hull (1988); Chen & Warren (1996).
    *   *Topics:* Datalog, negation, SLG/tabling, preventing combinatorial explosions in logic engines.
*   **Course 105: Proof-Carrying Code (PCC) & Integrity**
    *   *Readings:* Necula (1997); Appel (2001); Laurie et al. (RFC 6962).
    *   *Topics:* Tamper-evident logs, Certificate Transparency, BLAKE3 receipt chain architecture.
*   **Lab 100: Introduction to `Prolog8` & Micro-Kernels**
    *   *Practical:* Building bounded proof cells and byte-capped query structures.

## Year 2: Architecture, Execution, and Combinatorial Stress

**Objective:** Transition from theory to high-performance execution. Mastering WebAssembly and pushing systems to their combinatorial limits.

### Semester 3: High-Speed Substrates & Streaming Data
*   **Course 201: WebAssembly (WASM) as a Systems Substrate**
    *   *Readings:* Haas et al. (2017); WebAssembly Core Specification.
    *   *Topics:* JIT/AOT constraints, WASI, linear memory models, branchless execution (`bcinr`).
*   **Course 202: Streaming Process Mining & Sub-Millisecond Discovery**
    *   *Readings:* Burattin (2022); van Zelst et al. (2018).
    *   *Topics:* Real-time evaluation, SIMD-accelerated streaming DFGs.
*   **Lab 200: `wasm4pm` Architecture & Branchless Primitives**
    *   *Practical:* Profiling execution planners (`fast`, `balanced`, `quality`). Writing constant-time bitwise masking functions in Rust.

### Semester 4: Route Law and Actor Topologies
*   **Course 203: Actor-Oriented Execution & Supervision**
    *   *Readings:* Hewitt et al. (1973); Armstrong (2003).
    *   *Topics:* Erlang-style reliability, fault isolation, message-driven topologies.
*   **Course 204: Adversarial Boundaries & Counterfactuals**
    *   *Readings:* Clarke et al. (2001); Arias et al. (2018) [s(CASP)].
    *   *Topics:* Bounded model checking, adversarial probes (schema, lifecycle, cardinality violations).
*   **Milestone:** *Qualifying Examination (Written & Oral) focusing on architectural safety and combinatorial maximalism.*

---

## Year 3: Orchestration and Ecosystem Synthesis

**Objective:** Mastering MCPP (Multi-Agent Combinatorial Process Protocol) and preparing the dissertation proposal.

### Semester 5: The Full-Stack Orchestrator
*   **Course 301: MCPP & The AutoProcess State Machine**
    *   *Topics:* 8-dimensional RL state machines, Bellman self-reference constraints, MTTR observability, `autoprocess` state persistence.
*   **Course 302: Deterministic Machine Learning in WASM**
    *   *Topics:* `miniml-core`, arithmetic safety (MCC overflow, AUC handling), Rank-2 domain contracts.
*   **Lab 300: Orchestrating the Matrix**
    *   *Practical:* Deploying `MCPP` workflows that map POWL graphs against multi-object databases while emitting BLAKE3 receipts.

### Semester 6: Proposal and Deployment Paradigms
*   **Course 303: Socio-Technical Deployment & Automation Displacement**
    *   *Readings:* Acemoglu & Restrepo (2020); Chaves & Tsitsos (2001); Klinenberg (2018).
    *   *Topics:* Labor displacement, civic provision networks, the role of local institutions (e.g., churches/congregations) in absorbing automation shocks.
*   **Milestone:** *Dissertation Proposal Defense.* Students must propose a novel contribution to the `wasm4pm`/`MCPP` ecosystem utilizing the DSR framework.

---

## Year 4: Dissertation Research and Defense

**Objective:** Execute the proposed research, finalize the artifact, and defend the thesis.

### Semester 7: Empirical Benchmarking & System Hardening
*   **Independent Research:** Executing the combinatorial maximalist stress tests.
*   **Lab 400: The Compute Continuum**
    *   *Practical:* Deploying the candidate's artifact across Browser (`wasm32-unknown-unknown`), Edge (`wasm32-wasi`), and Cloud clusters. Gathering OTel and SPC metrics under CPU/memory throttling.

### Semester 8: Synthesis and Final Defense
*   **Dissertation Writing:** Synthesizing process evidence, route law, admissibility, and execution metrics into a cohesive thesis.
*   **Milestone:** *Final Public Defense.* The candidate must present their artifact, prove its mathematical and architectural safety, and justify its deployment within the Vision 2030 civic framework.

---

## Required Competencies for Graduation
1.  **Systems Engineering:** Fluency in Rust, WebAssembly, and strict memory management.
2.  **Formal Logic:** Ability to write and audit bounded Datalog/Prolog8 rules.
3.  **Process Science:** Deep understanding of OCEL 2.0, POWL, and declarative constraints.
4.  **Cryptographic Integrity:** Competence in building and verifying append-only, tamper-evident hash chains.
5.  **Design Science Research:** Ability to structure software engineering as verifiable academic science.
